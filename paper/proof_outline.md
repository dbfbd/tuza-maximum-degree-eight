# Tuza's conjecture for graphs of maximum degree at most eight

**Yang Xu**  
Draft of 27 August 2026

## Abstract

We prove that every graph of maximum degree at most eight satisfies Tuza's
triangle packing-covering conjecture.  The proof extends Gupta's recent
maximum-degree-seven theorem.  It uses Puleo's reducible-set framework, an
exhaustive census of eight-vertex weak König--Egerváry obstructions, and a
classification of the local graph around the endpoints of an edge incident
with a degree-eight vertex.  The finite part is accompanied by explicit
certificates and optimizer-free verifiers.

> Verification status: all 37,172 positive local certificates and all 208
> template certificates have passed standalone optimizer-free verification.
> Independent Z3 checks confirm the two exceptional local graphs and the
> redirected \(K_4-e\) core.  The orbit catalogue was regenerated byte for
> byte.  This is a public computer-assisted preprint and has not undergone
> independent reproduction or peer review.

## 1. Statement

For a graph \(G\), let \(\nu(G)\) be the maximum number of pairwise
edge-disjoint triangles and let \(\tau(G)\) be the minimum number of edges
meeting every triangle.

**Theorem.** If \(\Delta(G)\le 8\), then
\[
\tau(G)\le 2\nu(G).
\]

We use the theorem of Gupta that the conclusion holds when
\(\Delta(G)\le7\), and the reducible-set lemmas of Puleo.

## 2. Minimal counterexample

Assume the theorem is false and choose a counterexample \(G\) minimal under
proper-subgraph inclusion.  We may take \(G\) connected.  Every proper
subgraph of \(G\) satisfies Tuza's inequality.  Consequently \(G\) has no
reducible vertex set and no reducible edge set in Puleo's sense.

Puleo's argument now shows that \(G\) is robust: every component of every
link \(G[N(v)]\) has at least five vertices.  If a link belongs to the weak
König--Egerváry class (WKE), then either the vertex itself or a nonempty set
of its incident edges is reducible.  Thus every nonempty link relevant below
is non-WKE.

Gupta's maximum-degree-seven theorem implies that \(G\) has a vertex \(u\)
of degree eight.  Its link \(H_u=G[N(u)]\) has eight vertices.  Robustness
makes \(H_u\) connected, and irreducibility makes it non-WKE.

## 3. The eight-vertex link census

An exhaustive enumeration with `nauty-geng` gives 11,117 connected
unlabelled graphs on eight vertices.  Directly enumerating every matching
\(M\) and every set \(Q\) with \(|Q|\le |M|\) shows that 443 of these graphs
are non-WKE.

**Finite Lemma 1.** Every connected non-WKE graph on eight vertices has a
vertex of degree at least four.  In fact, exactly eight such graphs have
maximum degree at most four, and the minimum possible number of degree-four
vertices is two.  The unique graph attaining the latter minimum has degree
sequence \((4,4,3,3,3,3,1,1)\).

Choose \(v\in N(u)\) with degree at least four in \(H_u\), and put
\[
c=|N(u)\cap N(v)|=\deg_{H_u}(v).
\]
Then \(4\le c\le7\).  Also \(5\le\deg_G(v)\le8\).  Hence robustness and
irreducibility make the link of \(v\) connected and non-WKE as well.

## 4. The two-hub local graph

Put
\[
C=N(u)\cap N(v),\quad
A=N(u)\setminus(C\cup\{v\}),\quad
B=N(v)\setminus(C\cup\{u\}).
\]
Then
\[
|C|=c,\qquad |A|=7-c,\qquad 0\le |B|\le7-c.
\]
Let \(L\) contain the vertices \(\{u,v\}\cup C\cup A\cup B\) and all edges
of the induced graph except possible \(A\)--\(B\) edges.  Every triangle of
\(G\) containing \(u\) or \(v\) is a triangle of \(L\), and every triangle
of \(L\) is a triangle of \(G\).  Thus a local packing-cover certificate in
\(L\) proves that \(\{u,v\}\) is reducible in \(G\).

For each \(x\in C\), the maximum-degree bound gives the necessary budget
\[
\deg_{G[C]}(x)+|N(x)\cap A|+|N(x)\cap B|\le6.
\]

## 5. Finite local classification

The classification first applies the following general two-hub template.  If
\(R\subseteq E(G[C])\), let \(q(R)\) be the minimum size of a vertex cover of
\(G[C]-R\), and let \(p(R)\) be the maximum size of the standard two-hub
edge-disjoint triangle packing supported by \(R\).  Whenever
\[
1+|A|+|B|+|R|+2q(R)\le2p(R),
\]
the set \(\{u,v\}\) is reducible.  This criterion depends only on the core
\(G[C]\).

For cores not handled by the template, we enumerate all connected non-WKE
links of orders five through eight, distinguish the opposite hub, and join
the two compatible sides subject to the degree budget.  Isomorphic local
graphs are identified under simultaneous core permutations, independent
permutations of exclusive vertices, and hub exchange when the endpoint
degrees agree.

**Finite Lemma 2.** Under the conditions above, \(\{u,v\}\) is reducible
except for two local graphs.  Both exceptions have
\[
c=4,\quad |A|=|B|=3,\quad G[C]\text{ edgeless},
\]
and each exclusive side induces a triangle whose three vertices have the
same two neighbours in \(C\).  Up to isomorphism, the two cases correspond
to the two selected pairs in \(C\) having intersection two or one.

After removal of all template cases, there are exactly 37,174 compatible
local-graph orbits requiring an unrestricted reducibility search.  Of these,
37,172 have explicit certificates and precisely the two graphs just
described do not.  The endpoint-size breakdown is recorded in the supplied
machine-readable census; explicitly:

| \(c\) | \(|A|\) | possible \(|B|\) and number of solver orbits |
|---:|---:|:---|
| 4 | 3 | 0: 141; 1: 144; 2: 10,417; 3: 7,991 |
| 5 | 2 | 0: 24; 1: 3,652; 2: 11,964 |
| 6 | 1 | 0: 56; 1: 2,783 |
| 7 | 0 | 0: 2 |

Each positive certificate consists of an edge-disjoint triangle set \(S\)
and an edge set \(X\) such that:

1. \(|X|\le2|S|\);
2. \(X\) meets every triangle containing \(u\) or \(v\);
3. \(X\) contains every edge of a triangle in \(S\) whose endpoints both
   lie outside \(\{u,v\}\).

These conditions are checked by a standalone verifier that uses no optimizer.
An independent Z3 encoding proves that neither exceptional local graph has
such a two-hub certificate.

## 6. Eliminating the exceptions

In either exceptional graph, choose the specified vertex \(a\in A\).  Direct
inspection gives
\[
|N(u)\cap N(a)|=4
\]
and the graph induced by these four common neighbours is \(K_4-e\).

Apply Finite Lemma 2 again to the edge \(ua\).  The endpoint \(u\) still has
degree eight, while \(5\le\deg(a)\le8\).  Both endpoint links are connected
and non-WKE, and the same degree budget holds.  This new local graph cannot
be one of the two exceptions, since its common-neighbour core is \(K_4-e\),
whereas both exceptional cores are edgeless.  Therefore \(\{u,a\}\) is
reducible.

This contradicts the absence of reducible sets in the minimal counterexample.
The theorem follows.
